User Manual

Covers the planner, plus how to put its channel plan onto Betaflight, INAV and ArduPilot aircraft.

Planning estimates, not guarantees. The planner predicts interference from published-style figures and terrain data. Always confirm on site with a goggle or spectrum scan before launch. Transmit only on frequencies and power levels you are authorized to use at that location.

1. Quick start

The planner opens blank. You enter each team and its aircraft yourself.

  1. Pick the site in the top bar (MWTC, Washington DC, or Custom). The map moves there.
  2. + Add first team. Give it a name and its launch point: type an MGRS grid or lat/long, or press 📍 Pick on map and tap the spot. Then set the flying radius, flying height, and (optionally) start and end times.
  3. + Add aircraft inside the team card. Enter the callsign, pilot name, flight controller and goggles.
  4. Pick the VTX model, then ⬆ Upload VTX table file (or Paste table) with the table pulled from that quad (how).
  5. Set Takeoff / landing power and In flight power.
  6. Press + Add aircraft again for the next one. It copies the previous aircraft's setup, so you usually only change the callsign, pilot and table.
  7. Repeat for each team. Add known Wi-Fi or other emitters on the Environment tab.
  8. Press Solve channels and review the Plan tab. Fix anything red (what to do).
  9. ☰ → Flight controller commands: apply the settings to each aircraft (per firmware). ☰ → Channel card (print) for each team. ☰ → Save mission file to share.
Want to look around first? ☰ → Load example (this site) loads a complete 12-aircraft, 3-team plan. ☰ → New mission clears it again.

2. Installing & offline use

Mac and Windows

Open the planner's address in Chrome or Edge. Click the Install icon in the address bar (or browser menu → Install FPV Channel Planner). It then opens in its own window like a normal app.

To run it from a local copy of the folder:

python3 -m http.server 8765

…then browse to http://localhost:8765. (Opening index.html directly as a file will not enable offline mode.)

Android

The app must be served over HTTPS (any static web host). Open it in Chrome → ⋮ → Install app (or Add to Home screen). It runs full-screen and offline.

Preparing for no connectivity

  1. Open the app once while online. The program itself is now stored on the device.
  2. Pan and zoom the map to cover the mission area (include all teams plus a margin).
  3. ☰ → Download map + terrain for offline…, pick a basemap, press Download. Repeat for each area or basemap you want.
  4. Test it: switch the device to airplane mode and reopen the app. An offline badge shows in the top bar.

Your missions are saved automatically on the device. Use Save mission file to move a plan between devices (AirDrop, USB, email, etc.) and Open mission file… to load it.

If terrain did not download for an area, the planner still works but treats those paths as clear line-of-sight (worst case). The Teams and Environment tabs say when this is happening.

3. Key concepts

TermWhat it means for planning
Channel spacingAn analog video signal is about 18–20 MHz wide. Pilots in the same area want at least ~37 MHz between channels (Raceband spacing); closer than that and you see lines or a second picture.
Near–farA strong VTX close to your goggles can swamp you even on a different channel, because receiver filters only reject so much. The closer and more powerful the other drone, the more spacing you need. This is the main reason distance and terrain matter.
Intermodulation (IMD)Two strong signals mix inside your receiver and create a ghost at 2×f₁ − f₂. Three channels can each be well spaced and still have one VTX's ghost land on a third pilot. The solver checks every pair.
Terrain shieldingA ridge between two teams can block 20–60 dB of interference, which often lets them reuse channels. Open valleys and urban rooftops do the opposite.
PolarizationRHCP vs LHCP antennas reject each other by roughly 15 dB on a clear line of sight (much less once the signal bounces). Giving neighbouring teams opposite polarization is a cheap extra margin.
Time windowsTeams that are never airborne at the same time cannot interfere, so they can share channels.
HDZero (digital)Uses the same 5.8 GHz channels as analog but a wider signal (~27 MHz). It interferes with analog and vice versa, so plan it together.

4. Teams & Aircraft tab

Everything about who is flying what, and where, is entered here. Each team is a card, and its aircraft are listed inside it.

+ Add aircraft copies the previous aircraft in the team: VTX, table, powers, firmware, goggles and antennas. Only the callsign and pilot are cleared. A copied table is marked copied table until you upload that quad's own.

5. Uploading VTX tables

A VTX table lists the exact frequencies and power levels a quad's VTX is configured for. Upload one for every aircraft. The presets are approximate templates only.

From a Betaflight quad

  1. Connect the quad to Betaflight Configurator → CLI tab.
  2. Type vtxtable and press Enter.
  3. Either copy all the lines it prints (they start with vtxtable), then use the aircraft's Paste table.
    Or save them to a .txt file and use ⬆ Upload VTX table file.
    A VTX table .json file saved from Configurator's Video Transmitter tab also works.
  4. The aircraft row now shows table ✓ and the upload date.

From an INAV or ArduPilot quad

These firmwares have no vtxtable command; their channel tables are built in (see INAV and ArduPilot). The planner already restricts these aircraft to channels the firmware supports. What you still need is the VTX's power levels:

  • Pick the closest VTX model template, and check its power levels against the VTX's label or manual.
  • If they differ, use Paste table with a table you write yourself. Copy the band lines from the example below and change the power lines:
vtxtable bands 5
vtxtable channels 8
vtxtable band 1 BOSCAM_A A FACTORY 5865 5845 5825 5805 5785 5765 5745 5725
vtxtable band 2 BOSCAM_B B FACTORY 5733 5752 5771 5790 5809 5828 5847 5866
vtxtable band 3 BOSCAM_E E FACTORY 5705 5685 5665 5645 5885 5905 5925 5945
vtxtable band 4 FATSHARK F FACTORY 5740 5760 5780 5800 5820 5840 5860 5880
vtxtable band 5 RACEBAND R FACTORY 5658 5695 5732 5769 5806 5843 5880 5917
vtxtable powerlevels 4
vtxtable powervalues 25 200 500 1000
vtxtable powerlabels 25 200 500 1W

Power labels are read as mW (25, 200) or W (1W, 1.6W). Labels like PIT are kept but never planned as a flight power. A frequency of 0 disables that channel.

6. Goggles tab

Each receiver has a tunable range, filter quality (selectivity), noise figure, IIP3 (resistance to intermodulation), and the signal quality it needs for a clean picture (required SIR and SNR). The figures are estimates. If a receiver performs better or worse in the field, adjust them. Raising Required SIR makes the plan more conservative. Use + Receiver for goggles that aren't listed.

7. Environment tab

  • Noise rise — raises the noise floor for city RF clutter (DC defaults to 6 dB).
  • Extra loss when blocked — terrain data is bare earth (no trees or buildings). Leave at 0 to be conservative, or add 5–15 dB for dense forest or city blocks if you want credit for them.
  • Fade margin — safety margin taken off every pilot's own link (default 6 dB).
  • External emitters — anything on or near your band you don't control: Wi-Fi access points (5.8 GHz Wi-Fi channels 149–165 overlap the A, B and F bands), other units' drones, fixed video links. Add one, then give it a location (MGRS, lat/long, or 📍 Map) and set its frequency, bandwidth and power (EIRP). Common Wi-Fi APs are 20–30 dBm EIRP.

8. Solving

Press Solve channels (top bar or Plan tab). In a second or two the solver tries thousands of channel combinations from every VTX table and picks the one where the worst pilot has the most margin.

  • Also adjust in-flight VTX power: raises the in-flight power on links that are too weak, and lowers it everywhere else, while keeping every link at least 3 dB above its threshold. Lower power reduces interference and detectability. Takeoff power is never changed; aircraft with Lock flight power are left alone.
  • Allowed bands — untick bands you can't use (e.g. L band if your goggles can't tune it, or bands outside your frequency authorization).
  • Locked drones keep their channel and/or power; the others are planned around them.

Solving starts from random points, so two runs can give different (equally good) plans. Re-solve after any change.

9. Reading the Plan tab

Worst link margin is the headline: ≥ 3 dB OK, 0–3 dB marginal, < 0 conflict.

ColumnMeaning
Sig dBmYour own drone's signal at the edge of the team circle, after fade margin.
SNRSignal vs background noise. Low = range-limited.
SIRSignal vs all interference combined (other drones, IMD ghosts, external emitters).
MarginHow far above the "picture starts breaking up" point you are, combining noise and interference.
Limited byThe main cause when margin is low: a specific drone (with channel spacing), an IMD pair, an external emitter, or range/noise.

The spectrum strip shows every drone's channel and width, coloured by team (red outline = in conflict). The Pairwise C/I grid shows, for each pilot (row), how strong each other drone (column) is after filtering — red cells are the problem pairs.

Fixing a conflict

Limited by…Try
A teammate's drone at takeoffLower that drone's takeoff power, or launch further apart (increase Takeoff spacing only if you really will).
A teammate's drone (in flight)Increase In-flight spacing (keep airborne drones away from the pilots), fewer drones per team, or split the team into time windows.
Another team's droneMove a team behind terrain (watch the link colours), shrink radii, stagger time windows, give the teams opposite polarization, or lower power on the nearer team.
IMD A+BRe-solve; lock fewer drones; restrict bands so the solver uses a wider spread; lower power on the two named drones.
External emitterBan the overlapping band (e.g. untick A/B/F near Wi-Fi), or move the team away.
Range/noiseRaise in-flight VTX power, shrink the radius, use a higher-gain receive antenna, or raise the pilot antenna.

10. Exports

  • Channel card (print): one table per team with callsign, pilot, VTX, channel, MHz, takeoff → flight power, flight controller, goggles, margin, and a check-box column. Print it or "Save as PDF".
  • Flight controller commands — choose All drones or one drone. Commands are grouped by firmware (Betaflight CLI, INAV CLI, ArduPilot parameter file). Each sets the channel and the takeoff power, and either sets up the power switch or notes the flight power to switch to after takeoff. Download for a single ArduPilot drone saves a .param file. Apply them as described in section 11.
  • Save mission file — the whole plan, including your equipment library, as .fpvplan.json.

11. Flight controller setup

All three firmwares can set the VTX channel and power for you, provided the VTX control wire is connected and configured. The common pieces:

  • SmartAudio (TBS, Rush, Foxeer, GEPRC, most analog VTXs) and IRC Tramp use one wire from the VTX to a UART TX pin on the flight controller.
  • HDZero is controlled over MSP on the same UART as its DisplayPort OSD (follow HDZero's wiring guide for your FC).
  • A VTX with no control wire must be set with its button (and its LED/OSD menu). Mark it Lock channel and Lock power in the planner, set to whatever the button gives you, or set it to the solved channel by hand.

Takeoff power → flight power

The exported settings boot the VTX at its takeoff power. Getting to flight power once airborne is done one of three ways:

  1. Radio switch (recommended): enter the switch's RC channel under the aircraft's Advanced → Power switch RC channel. The export then adds the switch setup for that firmware (details in each section below).
  2. OSD or radio menu: the pilot raises the power from the goggles/OSD VTX menu or a radio Lua script after takeoff. The export lists the level to choose.
  3. Ground station: ArduPilot: change VTX_POWER from the GCS or OSD parameter menu.

Whichever you use, the planner's numbers assume the pilot really switches. A drone left at takeoff power is weaker on its own link. A drone that takes off at flight power hits nearby teammates much harder than planned.

Radio-side VTX control can overwrite the plan. ExpressLRS's VTX Administrator and TBS Crossfire's VTX settings can push a band/channel/power to the FC when the radio connects. Turn these off, or set them to the planned values, on every pilot's radio.

11.1 Betaflight

Betaflight (4.1 and later) needs a VTX table loaded before it can control a SmartAudio, Tramp or HDZero VTX. The band, channel and power numbers in Betaflight are positions in that table, which is why the planner wants each quad's table.

One-time setup

  1. Ports tab: on the UART wired to the VTX control wire, set Peripherals to VTX (TBS SmartAudio) or VTX (IRC Tramp). Save and reboot. (For HDZero, configure the UART for MSP DisplayPort per HDZero's Betaflight guide.)
  2. Video Transmitter tab: load the VTX table from your VTX manufacturer (Load from file or Load from clipboard), then Save. Many VTX makers publish Betaflight table files; Betaflight's wiki keeps a list.
  3. Confirm the tab shows the VTX as detected (device type and current frequency appear).

Getting the table into the planner

See Uploading VTX tables. In short:

  1. Betaflight Configurator → CLI tab → type vtxtable → Enter.
  2. Copy all the output lines (they start with vtxtable).
  3. Planner → that aircraft → Paste table (or ⬆ Upload VTX table file).

Example output:

vtxtable bands 5
vtxtable channels 8
vtxtable band 1 BOSCAM_A A FACTORY 5865 5845 5825 5805 5785 5765 5745 5725
vtxtable band 2 BOSCAM_B B FACTORY 5733 5752 5771 5790 5809 5828 5847 5866
vtxtable band 3 BOSCAM_E E FACTORY 5705 5685 5665    0 5885 5905    0    0
vtxtable band 4 FATSHARK F FACTORY 5740 5760 5780 5800 5820 5840 5860 5880
vtxtable band 5 RACEBAND R FACTORY 5658 5695 5732 5769 5806 5843 5880 5917
vtxtable powerlevels 4
vtxtable powervalues 14 23 27 30
vtxtable powerlabels 25 200 500 1W

Applying the plan

  1. Planner → ☰ → Flight controller commands → pick the drone → Copy.
  2. Configurator → CLI → paste → Enter. The save line stores it and reboots the FC.
# A1 (Team A) — pilot Alvarez — TBS Unify Pro32 HV — Betaflight
#   E2 5685 MHz · takeoff 25 mW → flight 500 mW
set vtx_band = 3
set vtx_channel = 2
set vtx_power = 1
# power switch on RC channel 6 (AUX2): low = takeoff, high = flight
vtx 0 1 0 0 1 900 1500
vtx 1 1 0 0 3 1525 2100
save

Power switch lines (only when a power switch RC channel is set). They use Betaflight's vtx command, which has the fields:

vtx <slot> <aux> <band> <channel> <power> <low µs> <high µs>
  • aux: 0 = AUX1 (RC channel 5).
  • band / channel: 0 = "don't change", so the switch only changes power.
  • power: a level number from the VTX table.

After pasting, type vtx on its own to list the active switch ranges and check them. Without a switch, the export adds a comment with the flight power level to select from the OSD or radio VTX menu.

SettingMeaning
vtx_bandBand number in the quad's VTX table, starting at 1. (0 = use vtx_freq directly.)
vtx_channelChannel 1–8 within that band.
vtx_powerPower level number in the table, starting at 1 (not mW).
vtx_low_power_disarmOFF, ON (minimum power while disarmed) or UNTIL_FIRST_ARM. Useful extra protection: grounded quads can't blast nearby goggles even if the switch is left high.

Verify

Reconnect and check the Video Transmitter tab (or type get vtx in the CLI). It should show the planned frequency. Then confirm in the goggles.

If the quad's table differs from the one in the planner (different band order, missing channels, different power list), the numbers will point at the wrong channel. The comment line above each block gives the intended MHz and power — check it against the Video Transmitter tab.

11.2 INAV

INAV does not use Betaflight VTX tables. It has a built-in table with five 5.8 GHz bands. The planner knows this: for drones set to INAV it only uses these channels (so no L band), and it works out the power level number from the VTX's protocol.

vtx_bandBandChannels 1–8 (MHz)
1A5865 5845 5825 5805 5785 5765 5745 5725
2B5733 5752 5771 5790 5809 5828 5847 5866
3E5705 5685 5665 5645 5885 5905 5925 5945
4F5740 5760 5780 5800 5820 5840 5860 5880
5R (Race)5658 5695 5732 5769 5806 5843 5880 5917

1.3 GHz IRC Tramp VTXs use vtx_frequency_group = FREQUENCYGROUP_1G3 with band 1 (1080–1360 in 40 MHz steps) or band 2 (same, but channel 5 = 1258 MHz).

One-time setup

  1. Ports tab: on the UART wired to the VTX control wire, choose the SmartAudio or IRC Tramp VTX option in the peripherals column. Save and reboot. (HDZero: VTX control runs over the MSP DisplayPort/OSD port — follow HDZero's INAV guide.)
  2. Optional CLI settings, only if the VTX doesn't respond:
SettingDefaultWhen to change
vtx_halfduplexONLeave ON for the normal single-wire connection.
vtx_smartaudio_stopbits2Set to 1 for TBS Sixty9 (SmartAudio 2.1).
vtx_smartaudio_early_akk_workaroundONWorkaround for early AKK SmartAudio VTXs.
vtx_softserial_shortstopOFFSome IRC Tramp VTXs on a soft-serial port need ON.
vtx_max_power_override0Tramp only: if the VTX reports the wrong maximum (e.g. says 200 mW but is 600 mW), enter the real max in mW.
vtx_low_power_disarmOFFON or UNTIL_FIRST_ARM recommended (minimum power while on the ground).
vtx_pit_mode_chan1Channel used in pit mode.

Power levels

vtx_power is a level number, and what it means depends on the protocol:

ProtocolLevels (vtx_power = 1, 2, 3…)
SmartAudio 2.1The VTX reports its own list (normally the same order as its Betaflight table). Check the mW on the OSD/Configurator after setting.
SmartAudio 1.0/2.0 (default list)25, 200, 500, 800 mW
IRC Tramp, 5.8 GHzDepends on the VTX's max: ≥800 mW → 25/100/200/500/800; ≥600 → 25/100/200/400/600; ≥400 → 25/100/200/400; ≥200 → 25/100/200
IRC Tramp, 1.3 GHz25 / 200 / 800 mW (25 / 200 / 2000 for 2 W units)
MSP (HDZero)1 = 25, 2 = 200, 3 = 500, 4 = 1000 mW ("MAX")

Set the VTX's Control protocol (aircraft → Advanced) so the planner picks the right level. If the planned mW isn't in INAV's list, the planner rounds down and marks the power with *.

Applying the plan

  1. Planner → ☰ → Flight controller commands → pick the drone → Copy.
  2. INAV Configurator → CLI → paste → Enter (save reboots the FC).
  3. Check the result in the goggles and OSD.
# C3 (Team C) — pilot Kim — TBS Unify Pro32 Nano — INAV
#   E4 5645 MHz · takeoff 25 mW → flight 400 mW
#   takeoff power: power level 1 as reported by the VTX — confirm it is 25 mW in the OSD/Configurator
#   after takeoff raise to power level 4 (400 mW) via OSD menu or an adjustment switch
set vtx_band = 3
set vtx_channel = 4
set vtx_power = 1
save

Switching to flight power in INAV: INAV has no Betaflight-style vtx switch command, so the export doesn't generate one. Do one of these instead:

  • Configurator → Adjustments tab: assign the "VTX power level" adjustment to a switch.
  • Or raise the power from the OSD menu after takeoff.

Check the adjustment name in your Configurator version. If you use vtx_low_power_disarm, the VTX stays at minimum power until armed.

You can also check or change these on the INAV Configurator's Configuration tab (VTX section) or from the OSD menu.

  2. On the serial port wired to the VTX control wire (example uses SERIAL5 — use your port number):
VTX typeParameters

ParameterMeaning

Applying the plan

  1. Planner → ☰ → Flight controller commands → pick the ArduPilot drone → Download (saves CALLSIGN-vtx.param).
  2. Mission Planner: CONFIG → Full Parameter List → Load from file → choose the file → Write Params. Reboot.
  3. QGroundControl uses a different file format. Set the four VTX_ values by hand under Vehicle Setup → Parameters (search "VTX").
  4. MAVProxy: param load CALLSIGN-vtx.param.
  5. Check the frequency in the goggles. ArduPilot announces the VTX band/channel at boot in the ground station messages.

Switching to flight power in ArduPilot: VTX_POWER is the power at boot, i.e. takeoff power. With a power switch RC channel set, the export also adds:

  • RCx_OPTION = 94 (VTX power switch).
  • VTX_MAX_POWER = the planned flight power.

ArduPilot spreads 6 power positions across the switch. The top position gives VTX_MAX_POWER.

The bottom position is pit mode (0 mW, no usable video). Use a 3-position switch: middle for takeoff, top for flight. The middle position's exact mW comes from VTX_MAX_POWER, so confirm it on the OSD before the mission.

Without a switch, change VTX_POWER to the flight value from the GCS or the OSD parameter menu after takeoff.

12. Field procedure

  1. Before departure: solve, print channel cards, save the mission file, download offline maps for the area. Apply and verify settings on every aircraft at the bench.
  2. On arrival: before anyone powers a VTX, scan the band with goggles or a spectrum analyzer. Add anything you find to Environment → External emitters and re-solve if needed.
  3. Power-up discipline: only power a quad once its pilot has confirmed the channel on the card. Take off at takeoff power, and switch to flight power only once clear of the launch point. Do the reverse before landing.
  4. Confirm each link: each pilot checks they see their own drone (wave a hand in front of the camera) and ticks the ✓ column on the card.
  5. Changes in the field: if a VTX fails or a team relocates, update the planner, re-solve with the unaffected drones locked, and hand out the changes.

14. How the model works

  • Own link: your drone at the edge of the team circle, line-of-sight, minus the fade margin.
  • Interference: every other drone airborne at the same time, at flight power, at its worst-case position (19 sample points across its team circle). For teammates the planner checks two cases and uses whichever is worse: a teammate's drone in flight at the in-flight spacing (flight power), and a teammate taking off or landing at the takeoff spacing (takeoff power).
  • Path loss: free-space loss plus diffraction over terrain (Deygout knife-edge method, ~30 m elevation data, 4/3-earth curvature). Terrain comes from AWS Terrain Tiles, which use USGS 3DEP in the US.
  • Receiver: off-channel rejection vs frequency offset (after subtracting VTX tolerance and allowing for wider digital signals), plus third-order intermodulation from every pair of strong signals, plus external emitters and noise.
  • Margin: combines the noise margin (SNR − required) and interference margin (SIR − required).
  • Solver: simulated annealing over every channel each drone's VTX table, receiver and firmware allow. It maximizes the worst pilot's margin, then the overall margin, with optional power raising and lowering.

Not modelled: trees and buildings (except the optional extra loss), reflections and multipath, antenna radiation patterns (full gain is assumed toward every interferer), and 5th-order intermodulation. Receiver figures are generic estimates.
